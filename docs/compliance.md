# Design information checks

Jurisdiction: New Zealand workplace plant. Checked 12 October 2026.

[WorkSafe upstream duties](https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/managing-health-and-safety/getting-started/understanding-the-law/upstream-duties/) explains designers' and manufacturers' duties to manage risks, conduct relevant testing and provide adequate information about intended purpose, test results and safe conditions of use. [WorkSafe position](https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/laws-and-regulations/operational-policy-framework/worksafe-positions/designers-manufacturers-importers-and-suppliers/) explains information sharing along the supply chain. These implement upstream duties under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. Read the current guidance for the actual design and jurisdiction.

| Check | Recorded evidence | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose missing | Intended use text | Documentation prompt linked to upstream information duties |
| Test evidence missing | Reference to testing or calculation evidence | Presence only, not validation of the evidence |
| Safe-use information missing | Instructions and conditions of use | Presence only, not an assessment of adequacy |
| Component not released | Every direct component revision is released | Local engineering policy, not a statutory release procedure |

Independent approval and immutable released revisions are local control choices, not claims that the law specifies this software workflow. Approval checks all four conditions; release checks them again. Changing a released design requires a new revision. The policy refuses obsolescence when any released assembly still depends on the component.

This system does not determine safe design, adequacy of tests, required standards, regulator notifications, product certification or the full duties under the Act. Engineers review actual evidence and assess risk. Evidence references can be incorrect, and an entered actor name does not authenticate the reviewer. Use reviewed authentication and database permissions for hosted operation. Australian, electrical, medical, aviation and other specialised rules require separate assessment.
